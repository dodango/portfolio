06Experimental fluid mechanics

Capillary spreading experiments

Bio-Inspired Fluid Flow Lab, Sep 2025 to Jun 2026. Undergraduate researcher

~4%
agreement with the lab’s baseline after power-law fitting
3 × 2
vessel diameters × surfactant concentrations (52, 85, 135 mm; 0.5 and 2.0 mM CTAB)

I studied how decane droplets spread across aqueous CTAB surfactant baths, comparing capillary (Laplace-driven) spreading below the critical micelle concentration with the much faster spreading above it, which was about ten times faster in our tests. I recorded the droplet spreading using a high-speed camera and extracted the spreading radius over time through frame-by-frame analysis in ImageJ and MATLAB.

The vessel-diameter sweep showed earlier plateauing in the 52 mm dish, consistent with boundary constraints; the report identifies wall meniscus pinning as a likely mechanism rather than a directly measured cause. The 85 mm and 135 mm vessels tracked closely over the main observation window, making the 85 mm dish a useful baseline and the 135 mm dish a larger unconstrained observation domain. I also designed and 3D-printed custom mounting structures to improve measurement precision.

Research report & experimental results

The accompanying ME 221 Advanced Viscous Flows proposal documents the experimental setup, vessel-diameter and concentration sweeps, data analysis, and proposed follow-up work. It also discusses the limits of attributing the smaller vessel’s behavior to wall effects without a direct measurement of the underlying mechanism.
