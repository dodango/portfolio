GR26 aerodynamics package

I lead the ground effects system for the 2026 car, coordinating a small team through concept, simulation, and manufacturing, and I designed the side diffusers myself (described in the next section). The intended aerodynamic package included a three-element front wing and a four-element rear wing. I co-engineered both designs in SolidWorks, including their mounts and CNC hot-wire-cut foam cores. Manufacturing constraints ultimately prevented completion of the front wing’s third element.

Compared with the GR25 baseline, the simulated GR26 package produced more downforce, a more forward aero balance, and a lower predicted package mass, at the cost of higher drag and reduced Cl/Cd. In lap simulations, the proposed aero package improved autocross time by 2.06 s and skidpad time by 0.50 s, with a 29.2% increase in lateral acceleration. These figures describe the design simulations, not performance achieved on track.

Ansys Fluent velocity visualization through successive cross-sections of an earlier GR26 design iteration. Tire wake can be seen entering the side-diffuser inlet; this finding informed later inlet geometry. Playback accelerated to 1.5×.

GR26 early iteration · Sequential velocity cross-sections · 1.5× playback

In the early flow visualization, disturbed air from the rotating tire was drawn into the side-diffuser inlet. I used this observation to revise the inlet geometry, making it taller and shifting it inward toward the car to reduce tire-wake ingestion. Subsequent GR26 diffuser CFD simulations showed improved flow behavior with the revised design.

The CFD runs in Ansys Fluent with the SST k-omega model on a 21.8 million cell poly-hex mesh, with rolling tires and a moving ground. I checked the structure with SolidWorks FEA and hand calculations, which gave a tie-rod buckling margin of 10.1, a rear wing fastener shear factor of safety of 3.8, and front wing fastener factors of 33.1 in tension and 18.4 in shear.

The wings use two-ply carbon skins over CNC-cut foam cores, PETG ribs, Nomex sandwich endplates, and waterjet-cut 6061-T6 aluminum mounts. Nose cone and body-panel work uses foam molds and fiberglass layups; laser-cut wooden templates support fabrication and tooling.

Design to competition

The final GR26 car differed from the simulated aerodynamic package. Unforeseen manufacturing difficulties prevented completion of the planned third front-wing element. Separately, concerns about the extra drag and its possible effect on endurance led the team to complete technical inspection without the wings. Because they were not included in the inspected configuration, the wings could not be used in dynamic events; the car competed without them.

For GR27, the team is addressing these lessons through earlier manufacturing planning and closer coordination between aerodynamic performance, drag, and endurance requirements. Side-diffuser fabrication planning is already underway, with resin infusion under investigation by a separate undergraduate research team.

I led the GR26 side-diffuser redesign and CFD evaluation, taking primary responsibility for the geometry and simulation work. An additional CFD design comparison predicted a 40% downforce improvement. The design uses a modified S-1223 diffuser profile and a BE 122-125 outlet element, with carbon-fiber tie-rod mounts, chassis attachment tabs, side skirts with a tire-wake deflector, and an XPS foam core in the outlet.

Iterative CFD and design-of-experiments studies targeted flow separation and aero balance. After tire wake was observed entering an early inlet, I tested taller, more inboard inlet geometries; subsequent GR26 CFD results showed improved flow behavior.

The diffusers were not manufactured for GR26 because of time, material, and funding constraints. GR27 manufacturing planning is now underway. A Gaucho Racing teammate leads a separate URCA research group of four first-year students investigating resin infusion as a potential fabrication process.

SolidWorks, Ansys Fluent, design of experiments (DOE), diffuser CAD and CFD
