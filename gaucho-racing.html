GR26 aerodynamics package

I lead the ground effects system for the 2026 car, coordinating a small team through design, simulation, and manufacturing. I also designed the side diffusers independently, as detailed below. The proposed package included a three-element front wing and a four-element rear wing; I co-engineered both assemblies in SolidWorks, including their mounts and foam cores, which were shaped by hand with a hot-wire cutter.

Compared with the GR25 baseline, the simulated GR26 package produced more downforce, a more forward aero balance, and a lower predicted package mass, at the cost of higher drag and reduced Cl/Cd. Lap simulations predicted that the proposed aero package would reduce autocross time by 2.06 s and skidpad time by 0.50 s, while increasing lateral acceleration by 29.2%.

Flow visualization revealed tire wake entering the diffuser inlet, prompting me to raise the inlet and move it inward toward the chassis. Subsequent CFD showed a more uniform velocity distribution and smoother, higher-velocity flow through the tunnels.

The CFD runs in Ansys Fluent with the SST k-omega model on a 21.8 million cell poly-hex mesh, with rolling tires and a moving ground. I checked the structure with SolidWorks FEA and hand calculations, which gave a tie-rod buckling margin of 10.1, a rear wing fastener shear factor of safety of 3.8, and front wing fastener factors of 33.1 in tension and 18.4 in shear.

Wing fabrication used two-ply carbon-fiber skins over hand-cut foam cores, PETG ribs, Nomex sandwich endplates, and waterjet-cut 6061-T6 aluminum mounts. Nosecone and body-panel work involved foam molds and fiberglass layups, with laser-cut wooden templates supporting tooling and alignment.

Design to competition

The final GR26 car differed from the simulated aerodynamic package. Unforeseen manufacturing difficulties prevented completion of the planned third front-wing element. Separately, concerns about the extra drag and its possible effect on endurance led the team to complete technical inspection without the wings. Because they were not included in the inspected configuration, the wings could not be used in dynamic events; the car competed without them. Gaucho Racing finished 30th overall at the 2026 FSAE Michigan competition.

For GR27, the team is applying these lessons through earlier manufacturing planning and closer coordination between aerodynamic performance, drag, and endurance requirements.

Side diffusers

I independently designed and evaluated the GR26 side diffusers using CAD and CFD. Isolated simulations predicted approximately 40% more downforce than the previous diffuser configuration.

A major design change was reducing the outlet angle from GR25 to mitigate potential flow separation, while incorporating a BE 122-125 outlet wing element to increase suction. Although CFD showed limited separation at the original angle, the redesign allowed me to explore the effects of diffuser geometry on aerodynamic performance. The design also incorporated a modified S-1223 profile, side skirts, a tire-wake deflector, carbon-fiber tie-rod mounts, and chassis tabs, with an XPS foam core planned for the outlet wing element.

The design progressed through manufacturing preparation, including chassis attachment tabs that were welded onto the car, but the diffusers themselves were not fabricated for GR26. Looking ahead to GR27, I’m helping newly recruited Gaucho Racing aero members investigate resin infusion as a potential manufacturing method through an undergraduate research project.
