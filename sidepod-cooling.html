Sidepod cooling research

UCSB Undergraduate Research Project (URCA). Design and Analysis Lead, Aug 2025 to May 2026. With Ryan Lee, Brandon Oliveira, and Aidan Kraus; advisor Prof. Emilie Dressaire

radiator mass flow in CFD, 0.148 to 0.216 kg/s
+81%
radiator air speed, 6.47 to 11.69 m/s
+63%
estimated time at max power before overheating, 11.0 to 17.9 min
$1,200
URCA grant secured

I led a team of four to win a grant for designing GR26’s sidepods around cooling. The sidepods were later removed from the car when the powertrain team moved to a closed-loop cooling system, and GR27 will not run sidepods either. The study is still useful as documentation: the CFD pipeline, the DOE dataset, and the manufacturing plan are written up and archived in case they are needed in the future.

We ran a manual DOE in Ansys Fluent over four radiator placements, each angled 70° from horizontal or vertical. Mass flow was similar across them, but two cases had much higher lift, so we chose Case B, which also had the lowest lift and fit the cooling loop layout. The chosen inlet captured 0.308 kg/s, and duct expansion and boundary layer turbulence reduced that to 0.216 kg/s at the radiator face. On the full car, the sidepods added only 1.623 N of lift and 0.322 N of drag, and the cooling subteam used the results in their lap simulations.

The manufacturing plan uses laser-cut wooden profiles, a hotwire-cut foam male mold, and carbon layup with vacuum bagging.
