Sidepod cooling research

UCSB Undergraduate Research Project (URCA). Design and Analysis Lead, Aug 2025 to May 2026. With Ryan Lee, Brandon Oliveira, and Aidan Kraus; advisor Prof. Emilie Dressaire

+81%
radiator air speed, 6.47 to 11.69 m/s
+63%
estimated time at max power before overheating, 11.0 to 17.9 min
$1,200
URCA grant secured

I led a four-person team in a funded undergraduate research project investigating cooling sidepods for GR26. The sidepods were ultimately removed when the powertrain team changed its cooling approach. Although the design was not implemented, the project produced a documented CFD workflow, design-of-experiments dataset, and manufacturing plan.

We compared four radiator-placement configurations using a manual design-of-experiments study in Ansys Fluent. Mass flow was similar across cases, but two generated substantially more lift. We selected Case B for its low lift and compatibility with the cooling-system layout. The chosen inlet captured 0.308 kg/s, with 0.216 kg/s reaching the radiator face after duct expansion and boundary-layer losses. Full-vehicle simulations predicted an additional 1.623 N of lift and 0.322 N of drag; the cooling subteam incorporated these results into lap simulations.

The manufacturing plan uses laser-cut wooden profiles, a hotwire-cut foam male mold, and carbon layup with vacuum bagging.
